Bundling solar panel cleaning with pigeon proofing means booking both jobs on the same roof-access visit instead of scheduling them separately, which cuts down on the labor and access costs that come with getting a crew, ladders, and safety gear up onto your roof twice. It also just makes sequencing sense: cleaning after proofing means the panels get washed right after any nest debris is cleared out, not before.

If you're weighing whether to do this in one call or spread it across two, here's the actual logic behind it, not just the pitch.

## Why one roof-access visit beats two

Getting onto a Tucson roof safely takes setup: ladders positioned correctly, harnesses if the pitch calls for it, a walk-through of the array to see what's actually there. That setup cost exists whether the crew is up there for twenty minutes or two hours. Split proofing and cleaning into separate appointments and you pay for that access twice, even if the actual labor on the panels is roughly the same either way.

Do them together and the crew that's already up on your roof, already has the ladder positioned, already knows how the array is mounted, just keeps working instead of packing up and coming back next month. That's the whole efficiency argument in one sentence: it's not a discount gimmick, it's fewer trips up the same ladder.

## Why the order matters: proofing first, then cleaning

There's a practical reason cleaning makes sense right after proofing, not before. If there's an existing pigeon nest under your panels, removing it as part of the proofing install kicks up debris — droppings, nesting material, feathers, dust that's been sitting under there for who knows how long. Cleaning the array right after the nest comes out and the mesh goes in means you're washing away the mess the removal actually created, instead of leaving it to bake in the Arizona sun until your next scheduled cleaning.

Do it the other way — clean first, proof later — and you've paid for a wash that gets undone the moment the crew starts pulling out an old nest.

## Two problems, one call

Arizona Pigeon Proofing sells these as two distinct services — custom-fit stainless mesh and deterrent work for proofing, deionized-water washes for cleaning — but the standing line on bundling is straightforward: bundle with proofing, save on the visit. That's not a claim about a specific percentage or dollar figure; it's a description of how the access savings work out when one crew handles both jobs in one trip instead of two separate ones.

## When bundling makes the most sense

Bundling is the obvious call in a few situations:

- **You've got an active nest or evidence of one.** Droppings, feathers, or nesting material under the panels mean cleaning alone won't hold — pigeons will just re-nest and undo the wash.
- **You're already scheduling a cleaning and haven't proofed yet.** If a crew is coming out anyway, adding proofing to that same visit avoids a second roof-access trip later.
- **Your panels haven't been proofed and you're overdue for a wash.** Monsoon dust and pollen build up regardless of pigeons, and NREL's research on rooftop solar has found that rain alone doesn't reliably clear that buildup — a scheduled wash still matters even with proofing installed.

Bundling makes less sense if your array is already proofed and clean, and you just need a routine seasonal wash — in that case, cleaning alone is the right call, no reason to add a service you don't need.

## An honest tradeoff to consider

Bundling isn't free of tradeoffs. If your roof only needs proofing and the panels were cleaned recently, paying for a cleaning you don't need just because it's convenient to bundle adds cost for no benefit. The efficiency argument only holds when both jobs are actually due. A crew quoting your specific array can tell you honestly whether bundling makes sense or whether one service alone covers what you need right now.

## FAQ

**Does bundling always cost less than booking separately?**
The savings come from paying for roof access once instead of twice, not from a fixed discount. A crew can give you the specific breakdown for your array.

**Should cleaning happen before or after pigeon proofing?**
After. If a nest is removed as part of the proofing install, it typically leaves debris on and around the panels that should be cleaned up — washing first just means paying to undo that mess later.

**What if I only need one service, not both?**
Then bundling doesn't help. If your panels are already proofed and were cleaned recently, or if there's no bird activity and no dust buildup, book only the service you actually need.

**How do I know if I have an active pigeon problem before booking?**
Look for droppings or feathers on or near the panels, and listen for cooing at dawn. If you're not sure, describe what you're seeing when you call — the crew can help you figure out if proofing is warranted before you book anything.
