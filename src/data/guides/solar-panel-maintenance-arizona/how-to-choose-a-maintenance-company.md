Look for a company with real Arizona experience, one that uses deionized-water cleaning instead of pressure washing, quotes a flat per-panel rate for your actual array, and can verify its reviews. Bonus points if it handles both cleaning and bird-proofing, since pigeons and dust are usually the same underlying maintenance problem. Get the quote in writing and expect a 24-48 hour turnaround — anything vaguer than that is a signal to keep looking.

That's the short version. Here's what each of those points actually means once you're on the phone with someone.

## Ask about Arizona-specific experience, not just "solar experience"

A lot of companies clean solar panels. Fewer have spent real time on Tucson roofs in July, or dealt with a monsoon cell that dumped an inch of rain and left the panels dirtier than before it started. Arizona heat, dust, and monsoon runoff behave differently than the "light rinse" conditions a lot of national franchise scripts are written for.

Ask two direct questions: How do you handle dust baked onto the glass by 110-degree heat? And what changes during monsoon season versus the dry months? A company that's actually worked here will answer with timing (cleaning after dust storms, not on a fixed calendar) and water chemistry, not just "we wash your panels." If pigeons come up unprompted, that's a good sign too — anyone who's worked Tucson roofs a while has seen what nesting under panels does to output.

## Ask what's actually in the wash — deionized water or a pressure washer

This is the single biggest quality differentiator in solar cleaning, and the one most homeowners never think to ask about. Deionized water has had its minerals stripped out, so it dries without leaving spots or streaks. Regular tap water evaporates and leaves mineral deposits behind, which is why some "cleaned" panels still look hazy afterward.

Pressure washing is worse than plain tap water. High-pressure spray can crack glass, loosen the frame seal, or damage the anti-reflective coating, and any of those can shorten the life of an expensive array. A legitimate maintenance company should describe a low-pressure, deionized-water process without you having to ask twice. If the answer is just "we hose them off," follow up.

It's also worth remembering rain alone doesn't solve the dust problem. NREL research has found rainfall isn't reliable for clearing pollen and grime off solar panels, which is exactly why a scheduled cleaning approach matters more in Arizona than in wetter climates. ## Find out if they handle bird-proofing, cleaning, or both

Some companies only clean. Some only do bird deterrents. Plenty of homeowners end up hiring two separate crews for two problems that are actually connected — pigeons nesting under panels leave droppings and debris that make the panels dirtier, and a cleaning crew that can't also seal off the space under the array will be back out in a few months for the same mess.

That doesn't make a single-service company a bad choice. A specialist who only does bird-proofing, done well, beats a generalist who does it poorly. But ask directly: "If I have both a pigeon problem and a dirt problem, is that one visit or two?" One call for both is simpler to schedule, and it's worth weighing that against whatever tradeoffs come with hiring two separate specialists instead. ## Get a specific quote, not a range

"$5 to $15 per panel" isn't a quote — it's a guess dressed up as one. A company serious about the work will look at your specific array (panel count, roof pitch, access, how bad the bird situation actually is) and come back with a flat per-panel rate for your job, not a range pulled from a rate card. Hold every estimate to that standard.

Turnaround matters too. A 24-48 hour window between your first call and a real quote is reasonable and achievable — roughly what a well-run local crew can manage without rushing the assessment. If a company can't say when to expect a number, or keeps pushing the timeline back, that's a scheduling problem now and probably a bigger one later.

## Check the reviews you can actually verify

Read the reviews for detail, not just star count. A review that mentions a specific type of work — cleaning and bird-proofing done in the same visit, for instance — tells you more than five stars and no text. Google reviews are easiest to verify since they're tied to a real profile; screenshot anything from a site you're not sure is legitimate.

For reference, here's the kind of detail worth looking for: "These guys did an amazing job on our solar panels! Not only did they clean the panels, install the bird proofing off the roof! ... They provided excellent service!! I highly recommend their services!!" That names the actual two-part job, which tells you more than a generic five-star rating with no context.

## FAQ

**Is pressure washing ever okay for solar panels?**
Generally no. The risk of cracking glass, damaging seals, or degrading the anti-reflective coating outweighs any speed advantage. Low-pressure, deionized-water cleaning is the safer standard.

**Do I need bird-proofing and cleaning from the same company?**
Not strictly, but it's often simpler. If pigeons are nesting under your panels, the debris they leave behind means you'll likely need both services anyway, and coordinating one visit instead of two saves scheduling headaches.

**How much should a solar panel maintenance quote cost me in time?**
A company with a real process should be able to quote your specific array within 24 to 48 hours of your first call, based on panel count and roof access.

**What's the difference between deionized water and tap water for cleaning panels?**
Deionized water has minerals removed, so it evaporates without leaving spots or mineral film. Tap water can leave a hazy residue behind as it dries.
