**Most Tucson-area homes need their solar panels cleaned twice a year** — once before monsoon season and once after — though the right frequency depends more on what's around your home than a fixed calendar.

## Why twice a year, and why those two windows specifically

Arizona's monsoon season runs June 15 through September 30, per the National Weather Service's official dates (set in 2008). Cleaning before it starts clears the months of accumulated dust that would otherwise mix with the first rains into a mineral film baked onto the glass by the sun. Cleaning again after it ends removes that mineral spotting along with whatever new dust settled during the calmer months that follow.

## What actually changes the frequency

- **Unpaved roads or agricultural land nearby**: dust accumulates faster than on a paved, developed street — some homes in these situations do better with three or four cleanings a year.
- **Panel tilt and roof pitch**: a lower tilt collects more dust than a steep one, since less of it slides off on its own.
- **Nearby construction**: active building sites nearby throw off enough dust to justify an extra cleaning outside the usual schedule.

## How much output is actually at stake

NREL research on solar soiling puts typical annual output losses around 4–15% for panels that go uncleaned, with short-term spikes well beyond that right after a dust event — enough to show up on a utility bill, not just a technical measurement.

Twice a year — pre- and post-monsoon — covers most homes in the Tucson area. If you're on an unpaved road, near open desert, or downwind of active construction, ask about a more frequent schedule when you [get a free quote](/quote/) for [solar panel cleaning](/solar-panel-cleaning/) in your city.
