Ground-mounted solar arrays are often at higher risk for pigeon nesting than roof-mounted ones, not lower. A ground mount sits at a height pigeons, rabbits, and ground squirrels can reach without flying at all, and the shaded gap underneath is just as attractive as the one under a roof array — with none of the height that keeps some birds away from a roofline. Ground mount solar panel bird proofing needs to cover the entire perimeter of the array, not just the rail edges, which is why it's usually a bigger job than people expect.

That's counterintuitive if you're picturing a roof as the more exposed, weather-beaten surface. But exposure isn't what pigeons are shopping for. They're shopping for shade, a stable temperature, and something overhead that keeps predators off them — and a ground mount checks every one of those boxes at a height that's actually easier to get to.

## Why ground mounts can be worse, not better

Ground-mounted arrays are common on the larger residential lots around Marana, Vail, and Sahuarita, where there's room to build a freestanding rack instead of putting panels on the roof. That extra ground space is exactly what makes the underside more attractive to wildlife.

**No flight required.** A roof array asks a pigeon to fly up and find the gap. A ground mount is reachable by walking, hopping, or a short low flight from a fence line or shrub. That lowers the bar for every animal that might use the space, not just birds.

**The same shade and shelter, at a more convenient height.** The physics underneath a ground mount are identical to a roof array: shade from direct sun, a solid panel overhead blocking rain and hawks, and a stable microclimate compared to open ground. Pigeons still nest there for the same reasons covered in our guide on [why pigeons nest under solar panels](/guides/pigeons-and-solar-panels/why-pigeons-nest-under-solar-panels/).

**More visitors than just pigeons.** A gap you can walk into is a gap rabbits and ground squirrels can use too. On a Tucson-area property, that often means chewed wiring and burrows dug right under the racking in addition to bird nesting, which is a wildlife problem a roof array simply doesn't have.

**More perimeter to defend.** A roof array typically has bird pressure at one or two accessible edges. A ground mount is exposed on all sides and often has open ends, so there's more linear footage of gap for something to get through.

None of this means roof arrays are risk-free — they're not, and the underlying attraction is the same. It means the assumption that ground mounts are somehow safer because they're "away from the roof" doesn't hold up once you look at what's actually drawing pigeons in.

## How proofing actually differs

The mesh itself works the same way in both cases: it seals the gap that gives pigeons and other wildlife access, without blocking the airflow the panels need. What changes is the shape of the job.

**Roof-mounted arrays** typically need mesh fitted and clipped along the panel's own mounting rails, sealing the edge of a raised rack that sits a few inches above the roof deck. The access points are limited to the rail perimeter of the array itself.

**Ground-mounted arrays** need skirting or mesh run around the full base of the structure, close to grade, often with the bottom edge secured or partially buried so ground squirrels can't just dig underneath it. Because the array is bigger and the perimeter is longer — and because you're dealing with digging animals, not just flying ones — the proofing has to be more robust at ground level than it would be along a roofline.

That's also why ground mounts are a poor candidate for a DIY fix. A roof array is a contained job with a shorter perimeter; a ground mount on a larger Tucson-area lot can run considerably longer, and any gap left at grade defeats the whole install. ## The bottom line

Don't assume a ground mount is the lower-risk option because it's not sitting on your roof. If anything, the ease of access makes it a more active target, and the proofing job is typically larger because of it. Both mounting types benefit from a professional install rather than a DIY patch, given how much perimeter is actually involved.

## FAQ

**Are ground-mounted solar panels more likely to attract pigeons than roof panels?**
The underlying attraction — shade, stable temperature, overhead protection — is the same for both. Ground mounts are often at higher practical risk because the space is reachable without flying, which also opens the door to rabbits and ground squirrels.

**Do ground mount arrays need different bird proofing than roof arrays?**
Yes. Roof arrays are typically proofed with mesh fitted to the mounting rails at the edge of the raised rack. Ground mounts need skirting or mesh around the full base perimeter, secured at grade so digging animals can't get underneath it.

**Can I DIY bird proofing on a ground-mounted array?**
It's possible in theory, but ground mounts usually have a longer perimeter than a roof array and need the bottom edge sealed against digging, not just draped over. Any gap left at grade undoes the whole install, which is why professional fitting is worth it on larger arrays.
