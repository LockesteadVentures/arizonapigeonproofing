The best window to bird-proof solar panels in Arizona is late winter through early spring — roughly February through April — before pigeons start nesting in earnest and before summer heat and monsoon storms make roof work harder. Getting mesh installed before nesting season means you're sealing empty space instead of removing an established nest first, which is faster, cleaner, and cheaper. If you missed that window, fall (after monsoon season winds down) is the next-best option.

## Pigeons don't wait for a convenient season

Pigeons in the Tucson area breed nearly year-round, but activity climbs sharply once the weather warms up in spring. A pair can raise several broods a year, and once they've picked the shaded gap under your panels as a nest site, they tend to come back to it. That's the core problem with waiting: an empty spot under a solar array looks the same in January whether or not pigeons plan to move in come March. Once eggs or chicks are present, removal gets more involved — nests have to be cleared out, the area gets a proper cleaning, and only then can the mesh go on. Doing that same job before any nesting activity starts is simpler on every front: less debris, no birds to relocate, and no need to time the work around a nesting cycle already in progress.

This is really a prevention-versus-remediation question. Prevention is a mesh install on a clean, empty roof. Remediation is a nest removal, a cleanup, and then the same mesh install — more labor, more mess, and often more cost. If you've already seen pigeons scouting your roofline, that's your cue to act now rather than wait for "someday."

## Why Arizona's climate actually dictates the calendar

Timing bird-proofing isn't just about pigeon behavior — the desert climate itself narrows the practical window for the work.

**Monsoon season (roughly July through September) is a bad time for exterior roof work.** Afternoon storms roll in fast, wind gusts pick up loose debris, and a crew working on a roof during a lightning-prone monsoon cell is a safety problem, not just a scheduling inconvenience. Installs scheduled during peak monsoon weeks get rescheduled more often, which slows everyone down.

**Peak summer heat is hard on both people and materials.** A Tucson roof in July can sit well above 150°F on dark shingles or membrane by early afternoon. That's a real safety issue for anyone working up there for hours, and it affects the install itself — adhesives and sealants used to anchor mesh brackets cure differently in extreme heat, and installers have to work around the worst of the afternoon sun rather than through it. None of this makes summer installs impossible, but it does make them slower and less pleasant for everyone involved.

**Late winter into early spring is the sweet spot.** Mild daytime temperatures mean a crew can work a full day without racing the heat. It's dry, with none of the storm-delay risk of monsoon season. And critically, it lands before pigeon nesting activity ramps up for the year — so the mesh is in place before there's anything to remove.

If early spring has already passed you by, don't write off the whole year. Fall, once monsoon season tapers off and before winter's shorter days, is a solid second window — mild weather, no storms, and still ahead of the following spring's nesting push.

Since a bird-proofing crew is already on your roof, it's worth pairing the visit with a solar panel cleaning appointment — especially if you're doing the work in that late-winter-to-spring window, right before monsoon season brings dust storms and, eventually, rain that (per NREL's research) doesn't actually rinse panels clean on its own. One visit, two problems solved, and your system heads into the hottest, dustiest stretch of the year already protected and running at full output. That's the logic behind doing pigeon proofing and cleaning as a single call rather than two separate projects months apart.

## FAQ

**Is winter too cold to bird-proof solar panels in Tucson?**
No. Tucson winters are mild by any standard, and daytime temperatures are comfortable for roof work. Winter through early spring is one of the best stretches of the year for this job.

**Can bird-proofing happen during monsoon season if that's the only time that works?**
It can, but expect more scheduling flexibility to be needed. Storm cells can delay or reschedule a visit for safety reasons, and installers may need to work around active weather rather than a full clear day.

**What if pigeons are already nesting under my panels right now?**
Don't wait for a "better" season. An active nest should be addressed as soon as possible — the longer it sits, the more mess and potential panel damage accumulates. Nest removal and mesh installation can happen together in one visit regardless of time of year.
