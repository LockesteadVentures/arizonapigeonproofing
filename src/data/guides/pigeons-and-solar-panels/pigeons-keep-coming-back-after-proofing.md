If pigeons are still nesting after bird proofing, the first install almost always has a physical gap somewhere, not a pigeon problem that's unsolvable. The usual culprits are unsealed corners, draped netting instead of rail-mounted mesh, an old nest that got sealed in instead of removed first, or cheap plastic mesh that's degraded in the sun. Find which one happened, fix it properly, and a second attempt almost always holds for good.

That's worth saying up front, because it's easy to assume round two means the birds "win" or that pigeon proofing doesn't work. It's neither. The first job left an opening, and pigeons are very good at finding openings.

## Why the First Attempt Usually Fails

Pigeons don't need much. About an inch of gap is enough for a bird to squeeze through, and they test edges the way water finds a crack. A proofing job that's 95% sealed isn't mostly working — it's fully open, because the birds only need the 5%.

**Gaps at corners and edges where mesh wasn't fully sealed to the rail.** This is the single most common failure point. Mesh has to be cut and fitted to the exact profile of the mounting rail, then clipped tight at every corner. A straight run along a panel edge is easy to get right, but the turn at a corner requires overlap or a custom cut, and a rushed job leaves a small triangular gap exactly there — often invisible from the ground.

**Draped netting instead of rail-mounted mesh.** Netting laid loosely over the top of an array, rather than mesh fitted and clipped to the rails, moves. Monsoon wind lifts an edge, and the netting shifts a few inches over a season of heat expansion and contraction. Pigeons don't need to break through the material — they just need to find the edge that's come loose, which is what draped netting produces over time almost by design.

**DIY installs done without removing an existing nest first.** If birds were already living under the panels before proofing went up, and the nest and debris weren't cleared out before sealing, the install traps the problem instead of solving it. The same pair, or a new one, tends to return to the exact same spot the moment any gap reopens.

**Settling and UV degradation of cheaper plastic materials.** Arizona sun is hard on plastic. Lower-grade mesh or clips get brittle and start to crack or shrink within a season or two of full sun exposure, especially on south- and west-facing roof slopes. An install that looked solid on day one can have compromised material by month eight — no single dramatic failure, just a slow give that eventually opens a gap.

## How to Diagnose Which One Happened

Before assuming you need to start over completely, walk the perimeter of the array and check each of these:

1. **Inspect every corner and edge up close**, not just from the ground. Look for daylight gaps, loose clips, or mesh that's pulled away from the rail.
2. **Check whether the material is mesh clipped to rails, or netting draped over the top.** If you can lift an edge by hand without tools, it's draped, not fitted — that's your likely answer.
3. **Look for old nesting material still present underneath**, sealed in rather than cleared out. Debris trapped inside means the first install skipped removal.
4. **Check the material itself for brittleness, cracking, or discoloration**, especially on the sun-facing side. Plastic that snaps or crumbles under light pressure has degraded past the point of holding a seal.

Most jobs turn out to have one clear cause once you actually look, not a mystery. It's rarely "pigeons found a way around good mesh" — it's almost always one of the four issues above, and usually visible once you're up close.

## The Honest Fix

A second attempt with proper materials and a proper install almost always solves this for good. Stainless mesh custom-fit to the mounting rail profile, sealed tight at every corner, installed only after any existing nest and debris is fully removed, doesn't share the failure points above. It doesn't sag, and it doesn't degrade in Tucson sun the way plastic does. This isn't a sales pitch so much as a straightforward mechanical fact: seal the gap correctly and there's no gap left to exploit.

## FAQ

**Why did my pigeon proofing work for a while and then stop?**
Usually gradual material failure or a slow-shifting gap, not a sudden one. Draped netting loosening over a season of wind and heat, or plastic mesh going brittle in the sun, both tend to fail slowly rather than all at once.

**Do I need to remove the whole install and start over?**
Not always. If the material itself is sound and the issue is one loose corner or edge, that section can sometimes be re-sealed. If the material is draped netting, degraded plastic, or there's still an old nest sealed underneath, a full redo with proper mesh is the more reliable fix.

**How do I know if there's still an old nest under my panels?**
Look and listen. Cooing at dawn, droppings accumulating again quickly, and visible debris at any gap edge all point to an active or trapped nest rather than a clean seal.
