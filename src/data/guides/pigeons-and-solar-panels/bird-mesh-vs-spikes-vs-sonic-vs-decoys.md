For pigeons nesting under solar panels, stainless steel bird mesh fitted and clipped to the mounting rails is the only method that reliably solves it long-term. Spikes and decoys do little against birds that just want the shaded, sheltered gap beneath your array, and sonic devices tend to lose effectiveness within weeks. Mesh costs more upfront and works best with a professional install, but it's the only option built for this exact gap.

That's the short answer. "Buy mesh" isn't useful advice on its own, so here's the honest breakdown. For the full picture on the under-panel nesting problem, see our [pigeon proofing guide](/guides/pigeons-and-solar-panels/).

Most bird deterrent advice online is written for open roofs, ledges, or signage, where birds land and loiter in the open. Under a solar panel is different: a dark, temperature-buffered, predator-shielded gap between panel and roof, usually four to six inches high, close to perfect pigeon nesting real estate in a Tucson summer. A deterrent has to work against nesting behavior in a sheltered void, not just perching on an open ledge.

## Stainless mesh fitted to rails

This is mesh custom-cut and clipped to the panel's own mounting rails, not draped over the glass. Done right, it seals the entire perimeter gap so pigeons can't get under the panel in the first place. No gap to check, no habituation curve, nothing for a bird to learn its way around.

It also doesn't block airflow the way people worry it might. The mesh sits at the edge of the gap, not across the underside of the panel, so the ventilation path that keeps panels cool stays open — see whether [bird mesh voids your solar warranty](/guides/pigeons-and-solar-panels/does-bird-mesh-void-solar-warranty/) if that's on your mind.

The honest tradeoff: it's the most expensive option here, and doing it correctly — measuring the rail profile, cutting mesh without gaps at the corners, securing it so monsoon wind doesn't work it loose — isn't a weekend project for most homeowners. It either goes right the first time or needs redoing. ## Plastic spikes

Spikes work well on flat ledges, parapet walls, and signage where birds land to perch. They do close to nothing for under-panel nesting, because the problem isn't a bird landing on a flat surface — it's a bird hopping into a shaded gap from the side or back edge of the array. Spikes along a panel's top edge don't block that entry point.

Arizona sun is hard on the plastic, too. A season or two of UV exposure and summer roof heat, and spikes get brittle, yellow, and start snapping off.

## Sonic and ultrasonic deterrents

These emit sound meant to alarm birds, sometimes randomized to slow habituation. In practice, pigeons learn fast that a repeating noise isn't an actual threat, and effectiveness drops off within weeks, sometimes days, once a nesting pair has already claimed a spot under a panel.

They don't hurt the panel or its warranty, but most units need a power source, meaning wiring routed near your array — an added point of installation a passive method like mesh doesn't require.

## Visual decoys

Fake owls and hawks rely on birds treating them as an ongoing predator threat. Pigeons figure out fast that a decoy which never moves, never makes a sound, and stays in the same spot isn't a predator. Effectiveness falls off within days to a couple of weeks, and a decoy does nothing to physically block a bird from getting under a panel. It's a deterrent by suggestion, not by barrier — cheap and a common first attempt, but it buys a false sense that the problem is handled while the birds get used to the fake bird.

## The honest bottom line

If pigeons are already nesting under your panels, or you've cleared a nest once and don't want to repeat it every season, mesh fitted to the mounting rails is the method built for that problem. Spikes, sonic devices, and decoys are reasonable tools elsewhere — an open ledge, a patio, a sign — but none physically closes the gap pigeons are using under your array. That gap is the whole issue.

The upfront cost is real, and a rushed or improperly fitted install can leave gaps that undo the point of doing it at all. It's worth having it fitted by someone who's done solar arrays specifically, not general pest-bird spikes or a big-box ledge kit.

## FAQ

**Do I need mesh if I've never seen pigeons under my panels?**
If your panels sit on standard rack mounting with a gap underneath and you're anywhere in Tucson Metro, the risk is ongoing. Mesh fitted before nesting starts is easier than removing an established nest later.

**Can I combine methods, like spikes plus a decoy?**
You can, but stacking weak deterrents doesn't add up to a strong one. None seal the physical gap, so a motivated pigeon will still get under the panel. Mesh is the only method here that removes the opportunity entirely.

**Does mesh installation affect how my panels are cleaned or maintained?**
No. Properly fitted mesh sits at the rail edge and doesn't interfere with cleaning or maintenance access.
