Skip the architectural review submittal where your HOA requires one, and the general pattern runs like this: a violation notice, a request to remove or modify the mesh, possibly a fine under the association's own enforcement policy, and a paperwork headache if you sell the house before it's resolved. None of that is guaranteed for your specific community, and plenty of HOAs never notice or never enforce. But weighed against the extra day or two it takes to submit a request first, the math almost always favors asking before the crew shows up.

## The realistic downside of skipping the request

Nobody installs mesh expecting trouble. Most homeowners who skip the HOA step do it because the install is small, the mesh is barely visible once it's on, and asking permission feels like overkill for something that isn't a room addition. That reasoning isn't crazy. But "unauthorized exterior modification" is usually defined broadly in HOA governing documents, and it doesn't take much to trip that wire.

**A violation notice.** This is the most common first step. Someone on the architectural committee, a neighbor, or a management company walkthrough spots the mesh, checks the file, finds no approved submittal, and a letter goes out. It's usually not hostile. It's a form letter asking you to either retroactively submit for approval or explain the installation.

**A request to remove or modify it.** If the after-the-fact submittal gets denied, or if the board decides the installation doesn't match what would have been approved (wrong mounting style, wrong color, wrong placement), you can be asked to take it down and redo it, or take it down entirely. That means paying for removal, possibly paying for a second install, and going without protection while pigeons have another season to move in.

**Fines, per the HOA's own schedule.** Many associations have a graduated fine structure written into their governing documents for uncorrected violations, and some apply per-day fines until the issue is resolved. Whether that happens, and how much, depends entirely on that specific HOA's enforcement policy. Some boards fine on the first notice. Others never get past a warning letter. There's no way to generalize this to "how much it costs" because it varies that much community to community.

**Friction at resale or disclosure.** An open architectural violation, or an installation that was never on file, can surface during a title search, a resale disclosure package, or a buyer's HOA document request. It's rarely a deal-killer, but it's exactly the kind of loose thread a title company or a picky buyer's agent flags, and it's an awkward conversation to have during closing instead of before installation.

## What the extra time actually costs you

Compare that list to the actual cost of doing it in order: submitting an architectural review request, waiting for a decision, then installing. Most Tucson HOAs process a routine exterior modification request within a couple of weeks, and some move faster for something as minor as bird mesh. A professional installer who works with custom-fit stainless mesh, rather than generic draped netting, can typically hand you a spec sheet describing the material and mounting method before your submittal even goes in, which is most of the paperwork a board wants to see. That's the trade: a short wait, versus the range of outcomes above if a board decides to enforce.

## Approval first is the practical, lower-risk path

We'd rather see a homeowner wait two weeks for a yes than deal with a removal order six months later. It's not that every HOA cracks down. Plenty don't. But you don't find out which kind of board you have until after the mesh is already on the roof, and by then the choice is gone. Submitting first costs you a little time. Skipping it is a bet, and you're the one holding the risk if it doesn't pay off.

## FAQ

**Will every HOA fine you for installing bird mesh without approval?**
No. Some HOAs never notice, some send a warning and stop there, and others follow a formal fine schedule. It depends entirely on that community's enforcement policy, which is why confirming with your own board matters more than any general guide.

**Can I submit for approval after the mesh is already installed?**
Sometimes, but it's a weaker position. A retroactive submittal can still be denied, and if it is, you're paying for removal or modification instead of just waiting for a first-time yes.

**Does using a professional installer make approval faster?**
It can help. A crew that installs custom-fit stainless mesh can typically provide a spec sheet describing the material and mounting method up front, which is often exactly what a review committee wants to see before approving the request.
