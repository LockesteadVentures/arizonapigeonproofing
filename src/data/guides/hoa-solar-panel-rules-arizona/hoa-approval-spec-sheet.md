Most HOA architectural review committees want the same handful of things for a bird-mesh submittal: what the material is, how it attaches, what color it is, and whether it'll be visible from the street. Below is a sample spec sheet outline covering those points, built to hand to your committee as a starting draft. It is not a substitute for your community's actual submittal form, and your HOA's specific requirements may differ — more on that below.

Architectural review committees are volunteers reading submittals in their spare time, usually without a technical background in solar hardware. A vague request ("we'd like to install bird netting") tends to generate follow-up questions and a delayed vote. A submittal that already answers the obvious questions — what is it made of, how is it attached, will anyone notice it from the sidewalk — usually moves faster, because there's nothing left for the committee to ask.

## Sample spec sheet outline

**1. Project description**
A one- or two-sentence summary: installation of stainless steel mesh barrier around the perimeter of the existing rooftop solar array to prevent bird nesting, mounted to the array's mounting rails.

**2. Material specification**
- Mesh type: stainless steel woven mesh (not plastic netting or chicken wire)
- Aperture size: small enough to exclude pigeons while allowing airflow around panels
- Corrosion resistance: rated for outdoor exposure, UV and heat stable
- Expected lifespan: multi-year, non-degrading under direct sun

**3. Mounting method**
This is the detail most committees care most about, and the one worth stating in plain terms: the mesh clips or clamps to the existing aluminum mounting rails and racking hardware underneath and around the panel array's perimeter. It does not attach to the panels themselves, does not penetrate the roof, and does not require new roof penetrations beyond the array's existing mounting points.

**4. Visibility from ground level and street**
A short written description (or photo, if the committee prefers) noting the array's location on the roof plane, the pitch or slope facing the street, and whether the mesh sits flush against the roofline versus rising above it. For most Tucson-area installs, mesh sits low and tight to the array's edge, following the same roof line as the panels — it doesn't add height or bulk that would change the roof's silhouette from the street or a neighboring yard.

**5. Color and finish**
Mesh is typically available in a low-visibility finish (black or dark gray) intended to blend against the underside shadow line of the panels rather than stand out against roof tile or shingle color. Note the specific finish being proposed here, since some committees have color-matching requirements tied to the roof material.

**6. Installer and contractor information**
- Company name, license/insurance info, and contact
- Scope of work summary
- Estimated install timeline (most mesh jobs finish in a single day)

**7. Before/after documentation**
A note that photos will be taken before and after installation, useful both for the committee's records and for the homeowner's own file.

## Getting this documentation without building it yourself

Pulling together material specs, mounting diagrams, and finish details from scratch isn't paperwork most homeowners want to build alone. Professional installers who do this work regularly can typically provide spec sheet documentation directly as part of the job — the material spec, mounting method description, and finish details are standard information an experienced crew should be able to hand you for your submittal, rather than something you have to reverse-engineer from a product listing.

## This is a starting template, not your HOA's form

Every HOA sets its own architectural review requirements, and those requirements can include a specific submittal form, additional documents like a roof diagram, a required review timeline, or a formal vote at a board meeting. Some communities have solar-specific guidelines already on file; others handle it case by case. None of that is standardized across Arizona, and it changes over time as boards update their governing documents.

Confirm your own community's current process with your HOA board or management company before you submit anything, and use their official form if one exists. This article is general guidance to help you prepare, not legal advice, and it isn't a guarantee of approval.

## FAQ

**Does my HOA require a spec sheet for bird mesh installation?**
It depends on your community. Some HOAs require a formal architectural review submittal for any exterior modification, including bird mesh; others don't distinguish it from routine solar maintenance. Check your governing documents or ask your management company.

**Can my installer provide this documentation for me?**
Many professional installers can provide material specs, mounting method details, and finish information directly as part of the job, since it's standard information about the product and install method they use. Ask before you hire.

**Will mesh be visible from the street?**
Properly installed mesh sits tight against the array's rail line and follows the existing roof slope, so it typically doesn't add height or change the roof's silhouette. Whether it's visible at all depends on the array's location and your roof's pitch relative to the street.

**What if my HOA rejects the submittal?**
Ask specifically what concern drove the rejection — material, color, visibility, or paperwork completeness are the most common. Most rejections are fixable with a revised submittal rather than a flat no on bird mesh generally.
