Snowbirds and seasonal homeowners should schedule solar panel cleaning twice a year, timed to their travel calendar rather than the calendar on the wall: once shortly before arriving for the season, and once shortly before leaving for summer. That two-visit rhythm covers the two risks unique to part-time occupancy — months of production loss nobody's around to notice, and a monsoon season spent baking dust and pollen onto glass with no one home to catch it.

## The problem with an empty house

A full-time Tucson homeowner might glance at a solar monitoring app, notice output looking a little flat, and get the panels washed. A seasonal homeowner in Green Valley, SaddleBrooke, or Oro Valley doesn't have that luxury. If you're back in Minnesota or Michigan from May through October, nobody's checking the app, and nobody's looking up at the roof. A production drop that would get caught in week three at an occupied house can run quietly for five or six months at a seasonal one.

That gap matters more in the desert than most places. Fine dust builds a film on panel glass faster here than in most climates, and pollen from mesquite and palo verde adds another layer on top of it. NREL has found that rainfall alone doesn't reliably clear that buildup — a monsoon downpour can even streak it into a film that clings harder than loose dust would. So the assumption "it rained, the panels are probably fine" doesn't hold, and it holds even less when there's no one there to double-check it. By the time a seasonal homeowner arrives back in October or November, the array can have been quietly underperforming since spring.

## Clean before you arrive, not after

The fix is straightforward: schedule a cleaning to land right before you get back, not after you've settled in. If you're due in Tucson the first week of November, book the wash for late October. That way the system is producing at full capacity from day one of the season, instead of you spending the first few weeks of your stay running an array that's been dusty since June.

This timing also solves a practical problem. Most seasonal homeowners want their home in Tucson move-in ready — AC running, water heater topped off, and yes, solar producing what it should — the moment they walk in. A panel wash scheduled for the week before arrival gets that box checked without you having to think about it once you're actually there. It's one less thing on the list during an already busy transition week.

## Get a pre-departure cleaning before you leave for summer

The other half of the schedule matters just as much and gets skipped more often: a cleaning and quick inspection right before you head out for the summer. This does two things. First, it gives the panels a clean start heading into monsoon season, which is exactly when dust and pollen accumulate fastest and when nobody will be around to notice the buildup. Second, a pre-departure visit is a natural point to have a technician take a quick look at the array and the roofline — checking for anything that needs attention before the house sits empty for months, whether that's cleaning-related or a maintenance issue better caught now than in November. Skipping this step is how panels end up neglected all summer. The array isn't visibly a problem to anyone driving by, so it just sits there losing output through the hottest, dustiest stretch of the year until someone happens to check in October.

## Make it a habit: tie cleaning to your travel dates

The easiest way to keep this from falling through the cracks is to stop treating it as a separate task and start treating it as part of your seasonal routine, the same way you'd schedule a pest control visit or have someone check the mail. Pick your typical arrival and departure windows and book cleaning around them every year. Once it's tied to dates you're already tracking, it stops requiring any extra mental effort. For homeowners managing a property from a distance for half the year, that kind of low-effort, standing arrangement is worth more than a perfect schedule you have to remember to set up each time.

## FAQ

**How often should a seasonal home's solar panels be cleaned?**
Twice a year works well for most snowbird homeowners: once right before arriving for the season, and once right before leaving for summer. That covers both the arrival production drop and the unmonitored monsoon buildup.

**Why can't I just wait until I'm back in town to schedule cleaning?**
Because the panels have likely already been losing output for weeks or months by the time you notice, and nobody's checking a monitoring app while the house sits empty. Scheduling ahead of arrival means the system is already producing well when you get there.

**Is a pre-departure cleaning really necessary if the panels look fine when I leave?**
Yes. Dust and pollen film builds through monsoon season regardless of how clean panels look in spring, and rain doesn't reliably wash it off. A pre-departure visit also gives a technician a chance to flag anything else worth addressing before the home sits empty.
