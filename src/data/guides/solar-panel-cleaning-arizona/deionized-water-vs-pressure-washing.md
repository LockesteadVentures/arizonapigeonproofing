Deionized water cleaning is the only one of these three methods that leaves Tucson solar panels spot-free without physical risk to the panel. It works because the minerals that cause water spots have already been stripped out of the water before it ever touches the glass. Pressure washing can get panels visibly clean too, but it comes with a real risk of forcing water into seals and junction boxes. A hose and squeegee sits in between — harmless to the panel, but no match for Arizona's mineral-loaded tap water.

Here's how the three stack up, and why the method matters more than most homeowners assume.

## The comparison

| Method | Streak/Spot-Free Result | Risk of Physical Damage | Typical Suitability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deionized (DI) water washing** | Air-dries completely spot-free — no minerals left to deposit as the water evaporates | Low. Applied with soft brushes at low pressure; nothing forces water past seals | Routine professional cleaning for any soil level: dust, pollen, monsoon grime, bird debris |
| **Pressure washing** | Can look clean immediately after, but tap-water minerals still dry into spots once the surface dries | Higher. Can crack or lift edge seals, drive water into junction boxes, chip glass, or degrade anti-reflective coating | Not recommended for panel surfaces; most manufacturer warranties explicitly exclude it |
| **Hose-and-squeegee** | Leaves hard-water spots once the water dries, because Tucson tap water is mineral-heavy | Low pressure, but dragging a squeegee over grit can leave fine scratches over time | Fine for a quick rinse of very light dust between real cleanings, not for actual buildup |

## Why deionized water is the one that actually solves the spot problem

The mechanism is simple once you see it. Tucson tap water carries dissolved calcium, magnesium, and other minerals — that's what "hard water" means. Spray it on a hot panel and most of it evaporates before it can be wiped away. The water leaves; the minerals don't. What's left is the same white, chalky film you see baked onto car windshields and shower doors all over the Southwest, except now it's sitting on the glass your panels need clear to generate power.

Deionized water has been run through an ion-exchange process that strips those minerals out before it ever reaches the panel. There's nothing left to deposit, which is why a DI-fed wash can air-dry on its own, no squeegee or chamois needed, and still come out streak-free. It's not a stronger cleaner — it's water with nothing in it to leave behind. That's also why professional rigs pair it with a soft rotating brush at low pressure: the brush handles the dust mechanically, and the mineral-free water handles clarity. Worth noting: NREL research has found rain alone doesn't reliably clear pollen and fine dust off panels, which is part of why waiting on monsoon storms to do this job falls short.

## Why pressure washing is the one to be careful with

A pressure washer can blast dust off a panel fast, and that's exactly the problem. Panels aren't a driveway. The edges are sealed with a thin bead of silicone or rubber gasket material that keeps moisture out of the laminate. Concentrated high-pressure spray, especially held close or angled at a seam, can work water past that seal or straight into the junction box on the back of the panel — the exact spot where the electrical connections live. Get moisture in there and you're looking at corrosion or a ground fault down the road, not something you'll notice the same day. Pressure can also chip glass at close range or wear down the anti-reflective coating manufacturers apply to the front surface, which quietly reduces how much light reaches the cells. ## Where hose-and-squeegee actually earns its keep

We're not going to pretend a garden hose is useless. If your panels picked up a light coat of dust between real cleanings and you're comfortable getting on the roof safely, a gentle rinse followed by a squeegee pass is low-risk and can buy you some time. The honest limitation is Tucson's water itself. Our tap water is hard enough that the very same water leaves the mineral spots you're trying to avoid. You'll trade dust for streaks. And a squeegee dragged across grit, done often enough, can leave faint scratches that build up over years. It's a reasonable stopgap for very light dust, not a real substitute for a proper wash.

## FAQ

**Does deionized water need soap to clean panels?**
Usually not for routine washes. Most of the visible film on Tucson panels is dust and mineral spotting, and DI water alone handles both — the mechanical brush loosens dust, and the mineral-free water rinses without leaving new spots behind.

**Can I make my own deionized water at home?**
Small DI resin cartridges exist for home use, but the output volume and flow rate needed to properly wash a full array make this impractical for most homeowners compared to a professional rig built for the job.

**Is pressure washing ever okay for solar panels?**
It's not something we recommend for the panel surface itself, given the seal and junction-box risk. If a roof or nearby hardscape needs pressure washing, keep it well clear of the array.

**Why do my panels get spots even after rain?**
Rain in Tucson still carries dissolved minerals and dust from the air, and it doesn't rinse evenly across a tilted panel. NREL has noted that rainfall alone isn't a reliable way to keep panels clean, which is part of why scheduled cleaning still matters even in a wetter monsoon season.
