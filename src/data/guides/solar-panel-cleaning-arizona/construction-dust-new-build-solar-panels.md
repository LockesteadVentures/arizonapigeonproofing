If you had solar installed on a new build in a Tucson-area community that's still under construction, your panels are getting dirtier faster than a standard desert-dust schedule accounts for. Active job sites throw off cement dust, drywall dust, and fine graded-dirt particulate that settles heavier and stickier than typical blown desert dust. Until the grading and framing crews clear out of your neighborhood, plan on cleaning your panels more often than the general Arizona recommendation, not less.

This catches a lot of new homeowners off guard. You just bought a house with brand-new panels. Everything about the system feels new, including the glass. It's easy to assume new means low-maintenance for a while. In a growing subdivision, that assumption is exactly backwards.

## Why construction dust is different from regular desert dust

Ordinary Tucson dust is mostly fine, dry soil and pollen. Wind carries it, it settles, and it stays relatively loose on the panel surface until a good rinse or wash lifts it off. Active construction changes the mix.

Grading equipment and unpaved roads on a job site kick up a heavier volume of particulate than the surrounding desert produces on its own, and it happens closer to your roof if you're a street or two from active lots. Add in cement dust from concrete work and fine gypsum dust from drywall finishing, and you get particles that are not just more abundant but different in behavior. Cement and drywall dust tend to bind to glass more tightly than plain dirt, especially once even a little humidity or morning dew is involved. It can sit closer to the surface and, if left too long, be more abrasive to wipe away carelessly than a simple layer of dust would be. That's a real tradeoff worth naming honestly: build-up like this is more reason to get it professionally rinsed off with the right method, not a reason to grab a stiff brush and go at it yourself.

## The neighborhoods where this matters most right now

This is a live issue in Tucson's newer master-planned areas where homes are occupied street by street while construction continues nearby — Marana, Vail, Rita Ranch, Sahuarita, and Corona de Tucson all have pockets like this. If your solar went in when your block was one of the first finished, odds are good you've got active grading, framing, or paving happening within dust-carrying distance for months, sometimes longer.

## How to adjust your cleaning schedule

The standard Arizona guidance is to clean panels roughly twice a year, timed around monsoon season and spring pollen. If you're living next to an active construction zone, that schedule isn't enough. A more realistic starting point is checking your panels every 6 to 8 weeks while nearby building activity is ongoing, then stepping back down to the standard twice-yearly rhythm once the surrounding lots are finished and the heavy equipment traffic stops.

You don't need to guess at when to check. A visible haze on the glass, or a production number that looks lower than it did the month before, is your cue. That's also the exact reason new-build areas tend to see the sharpest early production drop of any Tucson neighborhoods: owners assume new panels are still in some kind of grace period and skip the first cleaning altogether, right when the panels need it most.

## FAQ

**How do I know if it's construction dust or regular desert dust on my panels?**
Construction dust often looks more like a dull, even film that doesn't wipe off easily with a light touch, sometimes with a slightly gray or chalky cast from cement or drywall particulate. Regular desert dust tends to sit looser on the glass. Either way, don't scrub it yourself — see the next question.

**Can I just wipe the dust off myself?**
You can, but cement and drywall dust are more likely to scratch coated glass than plain dirt if you use the wrong cloth or press too hard, and a garden hose alone won't fully remove it. A proper deionized-water wash avoids that risk.

**When should I go back to a normal twice-a-year cleaning schedule?**
Once the active construction near your home wraps up — grading, framing, and heavy equipment traffic in your immediate area — you can step back down from the every-6-to-8-week check to the standard monsoon/pollen seasonal schedule.

**Does this affect my solar warranty?**
Letting construction debris sit on panels for months doesn't void a warranty by itself, but it does cost you real production in the meantime. Cleaning them doesn't affect your warranty when done with a non-abrasive method.
