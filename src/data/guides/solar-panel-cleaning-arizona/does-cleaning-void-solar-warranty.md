Cleaning your solar panels does not void the manufacturer warranty. What can void it is *how* you clean them: abrasive scrubbing tools, high-pressure washing that forces water past seals into the junction box, harsh chemicals, or walking directly on the glass to reach a panel. Manufacturers warranty the equipment against defects, not against damage you cause during maintenance — so the method matters more than the act.

## What manufacturers are actually worried about

Read the fine print on most solar panel warranties and you won't find a blanket ban on cleaning. You'll find language excluding damage from "improper maintenance" or "unauthorized cleaning methods." That's a narrower target than it sounds, and it usually comes down to four things:

**Abrasive tools.** Wire brushes, scouring pads, or anything that can scratch the anti-reflective coating on the glass. Scratches don't just look bad — they create weak points and can reduce output over time.

**High-pressure washing.** A standard pressure washer, the kind you'd use on a driveway, can push water past the panel's frame seals and into the junction box on the back. That box houses the wiring. Water intrusion there is a real failure mode, and it's exactly the kind of thing a manufacturer will point to when denying a claim.

**Chemical cleaners.** Ammonia-based glass cleaners, degreasers, or anything with solvents can degrade the sealants and coatings panels are built with. Dish soap and a garden hose feel harmless enough, but even that's a step down from what manufacturers actually recommend.

**Foot traffic on the panels.** Walking on the glass to reach a far corner of the array is one of the more common ways homeowners (and, honestly, some cleaning crews) crack a panel or stress a mounting clip without realizing it in the moment.

None of that means cleaning is risky. It means *certain methods* are risky, and they happen to be the methods people default to because they're what's already in the garage.

## Check your specific manufacturer's guidance

Warranty terms aren't identical across brands. Some manufacturers spell out acceptable cleaning methods in the owner's documentation; others just exclude "improper maintenance" without much detail. Before you clean your own panels — or hire someone to do it — pull up your panel manufacturer's warranty document and look for the maintenance or cleaning section. It's a five-minute read that tells you exactly where the line is for your equipment.

## Why deionized-water cleaning avoids these problems

This is why professional solar cleaning services generally use deionized (DI) water systems rather than a pressure washer or a bucket of soapy water. DI water is stripped of minerals, so it doesn't leave the streaks or mineral spotting that hard Tucson tap water does — no towel-drying required, no residue. On the mechanical side, a DI-water wash uses soft brushes and low water pressure, not the concentrated force of a pressure washer, so there's nothing pushing water into seals or junction boxes. No chemicals touch the glass or the frame. And a crew working from ground level or an extension pole, not standing on the array, removes the foot-traffic risk entirely.

Put simply: DI-water cleaning is built around avoiding the four specific things that actually cause warranty problems, not around getting panels clean faster at the cost of the equipment.

Rain alone doesn't solve this either — NREL has found that rain doesn't adequately clear pollen and dust buildup from panels, which is part of why periodic cleaning matters here even though it can feel like a Tucson monsoon "should" handle it.

## FAQ

**Does a one-time DIY cleaning automatically void my warranty?**
Not automatically. A single gentle wash with the wrong tool might not cause damage at all. The risk is cumulative and method-dependent — the more abrasive or high-pressure the approach, the higher the odds of the kind of damage manufacturers exclude.

**Will my installer or manufacturer inspect for cleaning damage before honoring a claim?**
It varies, but if a panel comes in for a warranty claim with a cracked cell, scratched coating, or corrosion inside the junction box, that's the kind of physical evidence that can lead to a denial regardless of what caused the original defect claim.

**Is professional cleaning required to keep the warranty valid?**
No manufacturer requires you to hire a professional. What matters is the method, not who's holding the equipment. Professional DI-water cleaning just removes the guesswork.
