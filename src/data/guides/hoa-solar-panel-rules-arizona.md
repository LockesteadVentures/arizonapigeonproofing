Arizona law limits how much an HOA can restrict solar panels, but it doesn't eliminate the approval process — most associations can still regulate placement, mounting, and appearance through architectural review. In practice, that means your HOA can't say no to solar outright, but it can ask you to submit a plan and follow reasonable rules about how the system looks from the street. Adding bird deterrent mesh later usually falls into a gray area that depends on your community's specific documents.

This page is general information, not legal advice. HOA rules vary by community, get amended over time, and are enforced differently depending on who's on the board that year. Nothing here should be read as a guarantee of what your specific HOA will or won't approve. Always confirm current requirements with your own HOA board or management company before you install anything.

## How HOA approval typically works for solar in Arizona

Most Arizona HOAs handle solar the same general way, even though the fine print differs from one set of CC&Rs to the next.

**Submit before you install.** Almost every community requires an architectural review application before work begins, not after. That usually means a site plan or roof diagram showing panel placement, a product spec sheet, and sometimes a photo simulation of what the array will look like from the street or from a neighbor's yard.

**The board reviews for placement and appearance, not whether you're allowed to have solar at all.** Under Arizona's solar-access protections, an HOA generally can't ban solar panels or make the approval process so slow or expensive that it functions as a ban. What it can typically do is guide where the panels go (front-facing versus rear or side roof planes), how conduit and mounting hardware are concealed, and how the array reads against the roofline.

**Turnaround times vary by community and by state law minimums.** Some HOAs have a committee that meets monthly. Others review applications on a rolling basis. Arizona's solar-access statute sets some outer limits on how long an HOA can sit on a solar application, but exact timelines and any related fee caps depend on the specific law's terms and your HOA's own documents — check both before you submit.

**Bird mesh and other add-ons often need a separate look.** A lot of HOAs treat the solar array itself as pre-approved once the panels are installed, but a follow-up modification — like adding stainless mesh around the panel perimeter to keep pigeons out — can trigger its own mini-review, especially if the board hasn't seen that specific product before. This is one of the more common points of confusion we hear about from Tucson homeowners, and it's worth understanding before you schedule a bird-proofing install.

Every one of these points can differ by community. Some HOAs are relaxed about solar add-ons and rubber-stamp anything that isn't visible from the street. Others want a formal application for every bracket and clip. There's no way to know which kind of board you have without asking.

## Why this gets complicated with bird-proofing mesh specifically

Solar panels create a gap between the panel underside and the roof deck — exactly the kind of sheltered, warm space pigeons look for to nest. Once birds move in, droppings and nesting debris block airflow under the panels and can void manufacturer warranties tied to airflow clearance. Mesh installed around the panel perimeter closes that gap without altering the panels themselves.

The reason this can raise an HOA question at all: mesh is a physical addition to something already mounted on your roof, and some architectural guidelines cover any exterior modification, not just the original solar install. A custom-fit stainless mesh, installed low-profile against the roofline, tends to draw far less scrutiny than a loosely draped net that's visible from the ground. That's a genuine practical difference, not just a technicality — visibility is usually what boards are actually reviewing for.

## What the law does and doesn't cover

Arizona has a state-level solar-access law that limits how HOAs can restrict solar energy devices, similar to laws in several other sunbelt states. It's worth understanding the shape of that law before you assume either extreme — that your HOA has no say, or that they can block you outright. Neither is accurate. The law sets boundaries around HOA authority over solar equipment; it does not automatically preempt every architectural guideline a community has adopted, and it doesn't necessarily extend the same protections to secondary add-ons like bird mesh the way it does to the panels themselves.

## What to prepare before you submit for approval

Most HOA architectural review committees want to see the same handful of things regardless of what you're installing:

- A clear description of the product (make, model, material — for mesh, this typically means gauge, color, and mounting method)
- A site or roof diagram showing where it goes
- Confirmation that the install won't damage roofing material or void existing warranties
- Sometimes, a photo or rendering of how it looks from the street

Professional installers who do this work regularly can often put together a spec sheet formatted for exactly this kind of HOA submittal, which saves homeowners from having to translate technical mounting details into board-friendly language themselves. ## Do rules really differ by HOA?

Yes, meaningfully. Some Tucson-area communities have detailed, solar-specific architectural guidelines that spell out acceptable panel colors, frame finishes, and mounting heights. Others have older CC&Rs that predate widespread residential solar and leave the board to interpret general "exterior modification" language on a case-by-case basis. A few have updated their documents recently enough to address bird deterrent mesh by name; most haven't, which is exactly why review can feel unpredictable from one community to the next.

## The bottom line

Arizona's solar-access protections give homeowners real footing, but they don't remove the HOA approval step, and they don't necessarily cover every add-on the way they cover the panels themselves. The safest path is the boring one: check your CC&Rs, ask your management company what they need to see, and submit a complete application before any work starts, not after.

## FAQ

**Can my HOA legally stop me from installing solar panels in Arizona?**
Generally, no — Arizona's solar-access law limits an HOA's ability to prohibit solar energy devices outright. It can still regulate placement and appearance through architectural review, and the exact scope of that authority depends on the statute's language and your community's governing documents. Confirm specifics with your HOA before assuming either way.

**Does adding bird mesh to existing solar panels require a new HOA application?**
It depends on the community. Some HOAs treat mesh as a minor addition that doesn't need separate review; others require an application for any visible exterior change. See our dedicated page on [bird mesh and architectural review](/guides/hoa-solar-panel-rules-arizona/bird-mesh-architectural-review/) for what tends to speed this up.

**What documents does an HOA architectural committee usually want to see?**
Typically a product description, a site or roof diagram, and confirmation the install won't damage roofing or void warranties. Some boards also want a street-view photo or rendering. Requirements vary, so check your specific HOA's application before submitting.

**Is this page legal advice?**
No. This is general information based on how HOA architectural review commonly works in Arizona, not a legal opinion about your specific community. Rules vary by HOA and change over time.
