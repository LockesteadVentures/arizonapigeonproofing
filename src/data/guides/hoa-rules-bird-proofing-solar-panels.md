**Most HOAs allow bird mesh on solar panels without much friction, because it attaches to the mounting rails rather than the roof itself** — but whether you need a formal architectural review, and who reviews it, depends a lot on where in the Tucson area you actually live.

## It starts with who has jurisdiction, not just your HOA

Some Tucson-area communities sit inside an incorporated town with its own building department; others are unincorporated Pima County land where the county handles permitting instead, and any HOA rules sit on top of that as a separate, private layer. Neither one is stricter across the board — but they're genuinely different processes, and knowing which one applies to your address saves a step.

- **Incorporated towns** — Oro Valley, Marana, and Sahuarita each run their own building and planning departments, separate from Pima County.
- **Unincorporated communities** — Vail, Catalina Foothills, Green Valley, Casas Adobes, Flowing Wells, Tanque Verde, Corona de Tucson, and Picture Rocks all fall under Pima County rather than a town government. (Vail residents have voted against incorporating twice, most recently in 2023.)
- **Its own small city** — South Tucson has run separate city government, including its own permitting, since 1940 — despite being only 1.2 square miles.

## Guard-gated and age-restricted communities tend to review more closely

Communities like La Paloma, Ventana Canyon, and Skyline Country Club in the Catalina Foothills, and age-restricted communities like SaddleBrooke, SaddleBrooke Ranch, and Sun City Oro Valley, generally run a more thorough architectural review than a standard subdivision HOA — not because bird mesh is unusual, but because these communities review most exterior changes more carefully by design.

## What actually gets submitted

When an HOA does require review, it's usually just a spec sheet showing the mesh color, mounting method, and confirmation that it doesn't alter the roofline as seen from the street. That's paperwork we handle directly rather than leaving it to the homeowner.
